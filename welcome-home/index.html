The best part of coming home

The Welcome
Home Committee

Two cats. One dog. Seven very long days.

🐈 Marbs🐈 Biff🐕 Po

By late afternoon, Po had checked the front window forty-seven times.

“They’re coming,” she announced.

“You said that when the garbage truck arrived,” Marbs pointed out from the windowsill.

“And when a leaf moved,” added Biff.

“That leaf was suspicious.”

The family had been away for a week, which, according to Po’s calculations, was approximately sixteen years. Someone had provided meals and company, but that was different. Nobody smelled quite like her own people, and nobody knew the exact place behind her ears where the world became wonderful.

Marbs missed them too, although she preferred to describe it as a disruption in household staffing.

“The beds haven’t been properly occupied,” she said. “I’ve had to sleep on all of them myself.”

Biff rolled onto his back. “I miss the little people. They drop things.”

“They also cuddle you,” said Po.

“Yes. But sometimes they drop things while cuddling you. Those are the best ones.”

Marbs had organized a welcome-home plan. Po would remain composed. Biff would avoid climbing into the luggage. Marbs would greet everyone with dignified affection after allowing a suitable interval to suggest she hadn’t noticed they were gone.

“How long is a suitable interval?” Po asked.

“Three minutes.”

Po looked appalled. “They could leave again in three minutes!”

🐾 🐾 🐾

At that moment, a car turned into the driveway.

Po sprang up so quickly that her tail knocked a cushion off the couch.

“IT’S THEM!”

Marbs rose calmly, then lost her footing on the windowsill and landed in a flowerpot. She climbed out, sat down, and immediately started taking a bath, as though that had been her plan all along.

Biff was already at the door.

A key rattled in the lock, and the carefully prepared welcome-home plan collapsed immediately.

Po, the black-and-white miniature Australian Shepherd, became a furry celebration with feet. She greeted William, who was fourteen, then Eleanor, who was eleven, then returned to William in case anything important had happened during the previous two seconds. Her tail appeared to be trying to wag the entire house.

Biff rubbed against every available ankle and delivered a long, urgent report.

“You were gone. The couch was here. I checked your room. Marbs was bossy. I slept in your spot. You were still gone. I checked again.”

Marbs waited for her dignified interval.

It lasted almost half a second.

Then she climbed into Eleanor’s lap and began purring so loudly that nobody could hear the question, “Did you miss us?”

🐾 🐾 🐾

Later, the suitcases stood open, everyone was talking at once, and Biff had made himself comfortable on the clean vacation clothes.

“I thought you weren’t getting in the luggage,” Marbs said.

“I’m preventing another departure.”

Po lay across the children’s feet, touching as many of her people as possible.

The house smelled right again. The voices were right. Even the crumbs were right.

Marbs closed her eyes while Eleanor stroked her head.

“Well,” she murmured, “the staff is back.”

But she tucked her paw around Eleanor’s wrist.

Just to make sure.

A little welcome-home story for our family.
